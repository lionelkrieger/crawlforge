## Rationale

The site-type review found that the baseline spec has no handling for ASP.NET WebForms traps or for routing and navigation patterns of client-side applications, and that fragment discarding actively breaks hash-routed applications.

## Proposed text

**§5.2, current bullet:** "Scheme and host are lowercased; default ports are removed; fragments are discarded."

**Replacement:** "Scheme and host are lowercased; default ports are removed; fragments are discarded, except in hash-route mode (section 7.19). ASP.NET cookieless session segments (`(S(...))`, `(A(...))`, `(F(...))`) are removed from paths."

**§5.5 gains:** "Postback links (`javascript:__doPostBack(...)`, `WebForm_DoPostBackWithOptions`) are not followed. They are recorded as uncrawlable navigation with the source URL and control identifier."

**§7.12 soft-404 bullet gains:** "When a rendered document exists, soft-404 detection runs on the rendered document and takes precedence over the raw result."

**§7.18 gains:** "A difference between raw and rendered title, canonical, meta robots or hreflang is a high-severity finding. A rendered `noindex` absent from the raw document, or the reverse, is critical."

**New §7.19 Site-type rules:**

> | Rule | Behaviour |
> |---|---|
> | Hash routing | In rendered capture, a per-configuration hash-route mode preserves fragments starting `#/` or `#!` as distinct routes. Hash routing is always reported as a finding because search engines do not index hash routes. |
> | Non-crawlable navigation | In rendered capture, elements with click listeners or link/button roles that change the route without a crawlable `<a href>` are inventoried and reported. |
> | Client-side redirects | Navigations performed by script after load (frame navigation and history changes) are recorded as a distinct redirect type in the redirect chain, with timing, and reported. |
> | Service workers | Service workers are bypassed for every render; a registered service worker is reported as informational. |
> | Postback navigation | Uncrawlable postback navigation (section 5.5) is reported per page and per template. |
> | Cookieless sessions | Detected cookieless session segments are reported, since search engines index such URLs. |
> | ViewState weight | The size of `__VIEWSTATE` and `__EVENTVALIDATION` is measured per page and reported above a configurable threshold. |

## Open questions

1. ViewState threshold default (review suggested 100 KB).
2. Hash-route mode default off (proposed) or auto-enabled when hash routing is detected?
3. Optional "click discovery" mode that triggers non-crawlable navigation to find routes (labelled as beyond search-engine behaviour): in scope later, or not at all?

## Impact

Depends on CR-006 (CDP navigation and listener inspection). Fixture traps: postback link, hash route, client-side redirect, soft 404 on a client-side app, cookieless session URL (CR-015).

## Resolutions (owner, 2026-11-17)

1. ViewState threshold: 100 KB.
2. Hash-route mode: off by default; hash routing always reported with a suggestion to enable it.
3. **Click discovery: in scope now** (owner, citing difficulty crawling betting sites). Added as §5.6.4 with mandatory safety: non-GET/HEAD requests blocked during click discovery, destructive-action pattern list, no form filling, bounds and deduplication.
4. Wait rule amended so websockets, server-sent events, long-polling and other streaming requests do not prevent pages from settling (live-data sites such as betting sites).

Applied with these resolutions. Where the applied text differs from the proposed text above, the applied text in the architecture is authoritative.
